Picking Up the Guitar

My musical journey began in high school when playing guitar with friends pushed me to become more interested in music. As I continued learning, guitar became something I wanted to take more seriously.

I later studied music theory in college, which gave me another perspective on how music works. However, some of my biggest lessons came from actually working with other musicians.

Formal Intent

At around 19 years old, I joined my first band, Formal Intent. Being part of a band taught me lessons that I could not learn from practicing guitar alone. I learned more about performing, writing music, communicating with other musicians, and working toward a shared creative goal.

Discovering Music Production

In my mid-twenties, I became more interested in music production. At first, recording and producing music felt intimidating because there was so much to learn.

My friend and former bandmate Mike introduced me to Ableton and taught me some of the basics of recording and production. At the time, I did not expect music production to become such an important skill for me, but those early lessons gave me a foundation that I continued building on.

Honey & Virosa

My more recent experiences with Honey & Virosa pushed me to continue developing beyond simply playing guitar. I learned more about preparing music for live performances and working with backing tracks as part of a complete live set.

Looking back, each group of musicians I have worked with has taught me something different. Those experiences helped me grow not only as a guitarist, but also as a songwriter, performer, producer, and collaborator.

Still Learning

One of the most important things music has taught me is that there is always another skill to learn. My interests have grown from guitar into songwriting, recording, production, live performance, and music technology.

My journey through music is still continuing, and I am excited to see what I learn next.
